Animals at the edge

Six short profiles connect each animal to the habitat, pressures, and protection work surrounding it.

Field guide

Choose a habitat to narrow the collection.

Endangered

Asian elephant

South and Southeast Asian forests

Forest fragmentation separates herds from food, water, and traditional migration routes.

Protection focus: connected habitats and safer coexistence with nearby communities.

Critically endangered

Hawksbill turtle

Tropical coral reefs

Illegal shell trade, accidental capture, and reef loss continue to threaten this marine reptile.

Protection focus: safer fishing, protected nesting beaches, and healthy reefs.

Critically endangered

Sunda pangolin

Southeast Asian forests

This solitary, scale-covered mammal is heavily threatened by illegal wildlife trafficking.

Protection focus: stopping trafficking and protecting forest habitat.

Critically endangered

Amur leopard

Temperate forests of Northeast Asia

A small wild population depends on enough connected forest and healthy prey.

Protection focus: anti-poaching work, prey recovery, and habitat corridors.

Vulnerable

Snow leopard

High mountains of Central and South Asia

Prey loss and conflict with livestock owners put pressure on this wide-ranging cat.

Protection focus: community-led conservation and secure mountain habitat.

Endangered

Mountain gorilla

Montane forests of Central Africa

Its limited range makes habitat protection, disease prevention, and careful tourism essential.

Protection focus: protected forests and long-term cooperation with local communities.
